Personal Statement

Origin & Motivation

Growing up in South Africa during apartheid, and immigrating to the United States with my family, taught me resilience and adaptability early — lessons that have shaped every discipline I've pursued since. That foundation, paired with a lifelong love of learning, carried me first into graphic design and later into computer science, cybersecurity, and compliance work.

At Rasmussen College, I studied broadly across computer science: scripting, networking, databases, statistics, e-commerce, emerging technology, software engineering, and security. My coursework included inferential-statistics projects analyzing real-world data — earthquakes, infectious-disease spread, salary and job-title trends — work that sharpened both my quantitative reasoning and my ability to translate data into a clear narrative for people outside the field.

Teaching my nieces and nephews for over a decade taught me how to communicate clearly and hold someone's attention while they work through something hard. As a student, I leaned on a simple study philosophy — chunk it, chew it, change it — to memorize dense material daily, a habit that only got sharper working the deli counter at Publix, without internet or a computer on hand, where I had to reconsolidate what I'd learned purely from memory.

My motivation hasn't changed since: I believe technology and design, used well, make people's lives better. That belief is what pulled me from graphic design into cybersecurity and compliance work, and it's what keeps me there — a well-rounded foundation across art, design, computer science, and emerging technology, applied to solving real problems for real people.

Adapted from a 2023 graduate-school personal statement. The original named a specific graduate program and specialization that reflected my thinking at that time; this version keeps the personal story and the motivation, since both are still true, without restating an outdated program choice.

Technical Coursework & Capstone Experience

BS, Computer Science — Rasmussen College

My Computer Science coursework spanned scripting, networking, databases, statistics, e-commerce, emerging technology, programming fundamentals, operating-system architecture, software engineering, security, quality assurance, and web analytics — each area backed by real project work.

Project & Business Analysis

Built project plans and Gantt-chart timelines to track phases and flag risk across multiple case studies. Developed a communication plan for a simulated company acquisition. Wrote FAQ guides, statements of work, and milestone-based work plans for hypothetical client engagements in custom manufacturing, cloud services, and software contracting.

Web Analytics

Researched and wrote comparative reports on analytics tooling — including Google Analytics versus Adobe Analytics — covering ROI measurement, predictive analysis, data aggregation, and visualization. Wrote a web-design and UX best-practices report, and presented on making web analytics actionable at the Web 2.0 Tech Conference.

Software Engineering & Databases

Designed software activity and use-case models for an online-shopping system. Built a relational database management system for a retail e-commerce capstone project, including SEO research to support the sorting algorithm. Wrote a QA test plan for a financial-systems case study. Worked across CRUD operations, ER diagrams, and primary/foreign-key design in advanced SQL coursework.

Java & Applications

Built Java applications in Eclipse — including a checksum security tool, iterative and recursive algorithm timing tests, a FIFO application on Ubuntu, and an early build of what became my own project-management concept, TransitionTrack.

Emerging Technology

Wrote research reports on intelligent robotics, electric-vehicle automation, brain-computer interfaces, carbon nanotubes, biochips, digital twins, and deep neural networks — tracking how each technology might reshape everyday systems.

Operating Systems

Set up a Java virtual-machine environment on Ubuntu and documented the operating system's architecture end to end.
