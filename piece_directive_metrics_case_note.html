Performance Marketing Case Note

Reframing a Compliance Content Piece in Cost-Per-Opportunity Terms

A short case note applying performance-marketing metrics — not vanity metrics — to a piece of GRC content, to show the content is judged the way a revenue-focused agency judges it.

What this is: A speculative case note, not client work for or commissioned by Directive Consulting. I wrote it to demonstrate that I can think about content the way a performance-marketing agency does, using one of my own existing pieces as the example.

Why I built this for Directive's team

Directive's CEO Garrett Mehrguth has been publicly specific about what he thinks B2B marketers get wrong: too much attention on bounce rate, cost-per-click, and site-wide rankings, not nearly enough on close rate, cost-per-opportunity, and page-level authority. I already have a real content piece — my "NIST CSF Without the Jargon" article — and a working subscription funnel analytics dashboard. This note connects the two: what would it mean to judge that article by his stated metrics instead of the usual ones.

Two Ways to Judge the Same Content
Vanity Metrics
  • Site-wide domain authority
  • Total pageviews
  • Bounce rate
  • Cost per click
Metrics That Tie to Revenue
  • Authority of this specific page for "NIST CSF for non-technical teams"
  • Demo requests attributed to this page, not just traffic to it
  • Close rate of leads that touched this page vs. leads that didn't
  • Cost per opportunity generated, not cost per visitor
Applying It to a Real Page
QuestionWhy It Matters More Than the Obvious Metric
Who actually searches "NIST CSF explained simply"?Likely a compliance manager two levels below the CISO doing the buying — not the CISO. Write for the searcher, sell to the buyer.
Does this page rank for its specific target term, not just the domain overall?Page-level authority compounds; site-wide authority is a lagging indicator that doesn't tell you which content is doing the work.
What's the cost of producing this page vs. one sales-qualified opportunity it generates?Cost-per-opportunity is the number a CFO can actually defend content spend with — cost-per-click isn't.
"A page that ranks for the wrong term at a low cost per click is still a bad investment. A page that ranks for the right term at a higher cost per click, but produces one qualified opportunity, isn't."
What I'd Bring to a Content Seat at a Performance-Focused Agency

I built a working subscription-funnel analytics dashboard (CAC, LTV, churn, conversion-by-stage) as part of my own portfolio, so I'm not learning to think in these metrics for the first time on the job — I'm applying a habit I already have to a new content vertical.
