Positioning & Category Narrative Sample

From "Never Forget a Password" to "The Access Layer Every Enterprise Runs On"

A speculative category-reframe exercise — the kind of positioning work 1Password's marketing leadership talks about publicly when they describe turning an invisible consumer habit into a must-have enterprise category.

What this is: An unsolicited writing sample, not client work for or commissioned by 1Password. I built it after researching how 1Password's CMO, Melton Littlepage, and content/corporate marketing leadership talk publicly about category creation — to show I can write at that altitude, not to claim I've done this work for them.

Why I built this for 1Password specifically

1Password's CMO Melton Littlepage has spoken publicly (Dave Gerhardt Show, Marketing Today, Pipeline Visionaries) about the challenge of repositioning a well-known consumer password manager as a B2B category leader in enterprise security — moving from an "invisible problem" to a "must-have solution." Shannon Hughes helped build that same bridge from content into growth and corporate marketing during her time on the team. This piece is my attempt to write the kind of reframe they're describing, not to summarize their existing copy.

The Reframe
Same product category, two audiences, two entirely different value propositions.
Consumer Frame
"Never forget a password again."
Sells relief from a personal annoyance. The buyer is one person solving their own problem. Value is measured in convenience.
Enterprise Frame
"Give IT one place to see — and revoke — every credential your company touches."
Sells control and risk reduction to a buyer who isn't the end user. Value is measured in audit-readiness and breach exposure avoided.
Consumer Frame
"Your passwords, always in sync."
Feature-level convenience claim. Competes on ease of use against other password managers.
Enterprise Frame
"Every offboarded employee, every SaaS login, closed the same day — automatically."
Reframes the same sync capability as an access-governance control a CISO can point to during an audit.
"The strongest category reframes don't invent a new benefit — they take a feature the market already trusts and re-price its value for a buyer who's never asked for it before." — framing principle applied throughout this piece
Why This Reframe Works for 1Password Specifically
  • Trust is already earned. Millions of consumers already trust 1Password with their most sensitive credentials — that trust transfers to an enterprise buyer faster than a brand-new security vendor could earn it.
  • The buyer changes, the proof doesn't. The enterprise pitch doesn't need new evidence of security — it needs the existing evidence repackaged for a security team's risk register instead of an individual's convenience.
  • Category creation beats feature comparison. Positioning 1Password against "extended access management" as a category, rather than against other password managers feature-by-feature, is consistent with the category-leadership language 1Password's own marketing leadership uses publicly.
