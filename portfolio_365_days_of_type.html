The Practice
One Letter, One Artist's Style, Every Day

The Concept

Each session took one letterform and one famous artist's style and asked: what does this letter look like if that artist had drawn it? Working through the alphabet against a rotating cast of artists forces range — the same hand chasing one artist's sharp geometry has to shift entirely for the next artist's loose, gestural line a few letters later. Three hundred sixty-five rounds through the alphabet, never repeating the same letter-and-artist pairing twice.

In My Own Words

"The hard part isn't drawing a letter — it's disappearing into someone else's visual language long enough to make the letter look like they made it, then finding my own hand again for the next one."

From Letter to Wallpaper
Six-Artist Wallpaper Montages

The Concept

The individual letterforms don't stay individual for long. Once a set is finished, six artists' worth of letters are combined together into a single wallpaper montage — a repeating pattern built entirely from hand-lettering, where the pattern's texture comes from the tension between six completely different visual languages sharing one surface.

Why It Matters
Practice as a Design Discipline

The Concept

A daily practice like this isn't meant to produce a finished product — it's meant to keep the hand and eye sharp between paid projects, and to build genuine range across visual styles rather than one signature look. The habit of hand-lettering and illustration built here shows up directly in client-facing work: the children's picture book, editorial hand-lettering, and packaging typography elsewhere in this portfolio.
