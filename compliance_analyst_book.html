How to read this book

I am a Compliance Coordinator at Logical Coders in South Tampa, where I research governance, risk and compliance requirements for HIPAA clients. I graduate from Purdue Global with an MS in Cybersecurity Management in November 2026, and I bring more than twenty years of graphic design and production discipline to compliance work.
Every illustration in this book is my own. Each template page reads the same way:

Key IT auditing definitions
IT audit
A structured review of systems, processes and controls to confirm they protect information and work as intended.
Audit criteria
The requirement used to judge performance: HIPAA, NIST, contracts, policies or procedures.
Control
An action, process or technology safeguard that reduces risk.
Audit evidence
Reliable information that supports a conclusion: reports, approvals, tickets, logs, records.
Design vs. operating effectiveness
Whether a control is designed to address a risk, and whether it worked consistently all period.
Population and sample
The complete set of records, and the smaller group selected for testing.
Exception and finding
Evidence that missed a requirement; a finding documents issue, criteria, cause, risk and action.
Inherent and residual risk
Risk before controls, and the risk that remains after treatment.
EVIDENCE-HANDLING RULEPrinted on every template: no ePHI, passwords, private keys, full Social Security numbers or unnecessary screenshots in workpapers. Use the minimum necessary, redact, restrict access, store in an approved repository.

Audit Program

Risk-to-control audit planning
Definition. The auditor’s detailed testing guide. It connects each risk to a control objective, the criteria, a test procedure, the evidence, a sample and a conclusion.
CAPTION The System of Controls (2026)
How it works
1Risk identification asks what could go wrong.
2Control assignment matches each risk to a control that addresses it.
3Testing procedures check whether each control works.
4Evidence of effectiveness collects proof of the test results.
5Conclusion and opinion decides and reports.

When it fails
§ 404
Sarbanes-Oxley, 2002
Enron and WorldCom made this five-step audit law
After the Enron and WorldCom accounting scandals, Congress passed the Sarbanes-Oxley Act. Section 404 requires management to assess internal control over financial reporting, and larger companies need an outside auditor to report on it — find the risks, map the controls, test them, keep the evidence and give an opinion (PCAOB, n.d.-a; Sarbanes-Oxley Act, 2002).

The risk and control lifecycle
Definition. How the audit will be completed: objective, scope, people involved, timeline, evidence needs, testing approach, communication and final deliverables (ISACA, 2022).

Fieldwork Support Packet

The audit evidence journey
Definition. The central record for collecting, tracking and protecting audit evidence, so every item is complete, relevant, reliable, traceable and stored securely.
CAPTION Audit Evidence Journey (2026)
How it works
1Requested: a written request says exactly what is needed.
2Received from the control owner through the approved repository.
3Reviewed for completeness, accuracy and relevance to the control.
4Protected so only approved people can see or change it.
5Stored for as long as rules require — six years under HIPAA (§ 164.316).

Control Narrative

The parts of a control
Definition. Explains how a control works in practice: its owner, trigger, timing, actions, systems, evidence, exceptions, escalation and retention location.
CAPTION The Parts of a Control (2026)
How it works
1Trigger: the event or date that starts the control.
2Activity and owner: the task itself and the person who does it.
3Approval: a second person signs off that it was done correctly.
4Exception handling: what happens when it does not go as planned.
5Retained evidence: the ticket or report that proves it happened (COSO, 2013).

Risk Register Assessment

Inherent risk, treatment and residual risk
Definition. Identifies, prioritizes, treats and monitors risks. It records the risk before controls and the risk that remains after treatment.
CAPTION Inherent Risk, Treatment and Residual Risk (2026)
How it works
1Assess inherent risk before controls: likelihood × impact on a 1–5 scale.
2Treat it: mitigate, transfer, avoid or accept, weighing cost against benefit.
3Monitor residual risk, because new threats can raise it again (NIST, 2012).

When it fails
$1.4B
insurance claim

NotPetya damaged thousands of Merck’s computers in 2017. Merck claimed $1.4 billion under its property policy; insurers refused, citing a war exclusion. A New Jersey appeals court ruled for Merck in 2023 — insurance can transfer risk, but the exact policy language decides it (Merck & Co. v. ACE American Insurance Co., 2023).

Gap Analysis Report

Current state against the required standard
Definition. Compares the current state with a requirement and turns the difference into a practical, risk-based recommendation.
CAPTION Risk Management Journey: Gap Analysis (2026)
How it works
1Current process: how work is done today.
2Required standard: defined practices and connected systems.
3Identified gap: the missing pieces — procedures, data, tracking.
4Recommended action: how to close it. CSF 2.0 compares a Current Profile with a Target Profile the same way (NIST, 2024).

Definition. The central status record for requests, testing tasks, findings, actions, evidence, owners, deadlines and closure validation.

Operating Effectiveness Report

Control design review and sample testing
Definition. Documents whether a control was designed adequately and operated consistently during the audit period, based on defined test procedures and samples.
CAPTION Control Design Review (2026)
How it works
1Design review: read how the control should work and decide if it can stop the risk.
2Sample testing: check a sample of items against each attribute.
3Consistency and result: record whether it worked every time. Sampling lets the auditor conclude about the whole group (PCAOB, n.d.-b).

Account Lifecycle Review

Joiner, mover, leaver — stability through stewardship
Definition. Tests whether joiner, mover, leaver, contractor, shared, service and privileged access is approved, timely, appropriate and regularly reviewed.
CAPTION Access Management Journey (2026)
How it works
1Joiner: access is requested, approved and set up before day one.
2Mover: new access is added and old access removed.
3Leaver: every account is disabled on the last day.
4Recertification: managers confirm who still needs access (NIST, 2020).
